# cerave moisturizing cream: which size is the best deal per ounce, and how to stack cashback on top

CeraVe Moisturizing Cream is not a hard product to buy. It is a hard product to buy *well*. The 16 oz jar is stocked in roughly the same form at every chain in the US, and the sticker price swings from about $14 to $20 depending on which store you walked into and whether that store happens to be running its beauty week. A price tracker logged Walmart's listing at 4.7/5 across 26,226 reviews, which tells you the product itself isn't the question. The question is whether you paid $0.75 an ounce or $1.33 an ounce for the same white cream in the same blue tub.

That gap is what this page is about. Size math first, store-by-store reality second, and then the layer most price comparisons ignore entirely: the rebate you can collect on top of the shelf price.

## The formula is not the variable

The Moisturizing Cream has barely changed in years. The jar and the pump contain the same thing: a petrolatum-and-glycerin base with cetearyl alcohol, caprylic/capric triglyceride, dimethicone, three ceramides (NP, AP and EOP — CeraVe markets them as ceramides 1, 3 and 6-II), sodium hyaluronate, cholesterol and phytosphingosine, with MVE delivery technology doing the controlled-release work. Fragrance-free, paraben-free, non-comedogenic, allergy-tested, and it carries the National Eczema Association seal.

Two practical details worth knowing before you compare prices:

- The pump top and the jar lid are interchangeable. Costco's own listing describes the multi-pack that way, and it means the "with pump" version isn't a different formula, just a different lid.
- There is a 19 oz jumbo jar. When it's discounted it lands around $19, which works out to roughly a dollar an ounce — competitive with the standard 16 oz tub on sale, and better than full price.

So when you see two listings at different prices, you're almost always comparing packaging and timing, not product.

### What actually voids a rebate

Most rebate horror stories come down to a handful of avoidable moves:

- **Coupon aggregator sites.** Activating an offer on a different site mid-session can replace ShopBack's referral with someone else's. You only get one.
- **Gift cards.** Almost every portal excludes gift card purchases. Buying a gift card to pay for the cream breaks the chain.
- **Points that zero out the balance.** If you pay with rewards in a way that leaves no cash paid, there's no commission for anyone to share.
- **Excluded items.** Rate detail pages list exclusions. Read that line, not the headline rate.

On timing: cashback usually confirms around 90 days after the purchase, roughly when the retailer's return window closes. Confirmed cashback sits as a standing balance with no deadline to withdraw. ## Questions that come up before checkout

**Is the pump more expensive than the jar?**
At Walmart both versions list at $17.97. The grocery-channel jar can go lower on sale, which is why the tub sometimes undercuts the pump. The lid is interchangeable, so don't pay a premium for the pump.

**Is a 19 oz jumbo real?**
Yes, and when it's discounted it's priced comparably per ounce to the 16 oz tub on sale. It's a lot of product for a face-and-body cream, so it suits households rather than single users.

**Can you use it on your face?**
The label covers face and body, and the formula is non-comedogenic. It's a rich cream with petrolatum high in the ingredient list, so people with oily or acne-prone skin often find it heavy and use it at night instead of under makeup.

**Is the baby version the same?**
It's a separate 5 oz product formulated for babies, and it prices out at roughly $9.42 depending on the store — far more per ounce. Unless a pediatrician has recommended it specifically, there's no cost case for it over the standard cream.

**Does it help eczema?**
It carries the National Eczema Association seal of acceptance, which means it passed the association's review criteria for the ingredient and testing profile, not that it treats a flare. For moderate to severe eczema, that's a question for a dermatologist.

**Where's the cheapest place overall?**
Costco's two-pack on a manufacturer promo, with cashback tracked through a portal. If you don't have a membership, wait for Ulta's next $14.99 sale and stack a rebate on it.

## The short version

The CeraVe Moisturizing Cream price band is roughly $14 to $20 for 16 ounces, and most of that range is timing rather than quality. Two rules cover nearly every situation: buy the 16 oz in a multi-pack or a beauty sale rather than at list price, and route the purchase through a cashback portal so the rebate lands on top of the sale instead of replacing it. The 12 oz jar is the one format to skip, and a delivery app is the one channel that reliably costs more than walking into the store's own site.
